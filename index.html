02The research

One problem, end to end: from breath signal to spoken words, on hardware that costs tens of pounds.

Personalised Breath-Based Augmentative and Alternative Communication: Rapidly Retrainable Classification and Real-Time Embedded Deployment

For people with severe motor impairments, a controlled breath can be one of the few reliable voluntary signals left. My PhD builds a system that classifies individual breath patterns and maps them to communication intent, so a user can “speak” through breath alone.

Two ideas run through the work. First, personalisation: the classifier retrains rapidly on a small amount of a new user’s own breath data, rather than expecting one model to fit everyone. Second, embedded deployment: the whole pipeline runs in real time on a Raspberry Pi 5, with sub-second predictions, instead of depending on a laptop or the cloud.

The research is supervised by Dr James Fleming and Dr Sijung Hu at the Wolfson School of Mechanical, Electrical and Manufacturing Engineering, and developed in partnership with the Ace Centre, the charity for assistive technology and AAC.

This research line was started by Dr Yasmin Elsahar. My own work within it focuses on the AI and machine learning and the electronics / embedded systems.
